Fundamental Concept

Why Put an Analog Signal on a Carrier?

Antenna quarter-wavelength physics, band-pass physical media, and frequency sharing
Slide 3 / 23
📡 The Physical Antenna Problem

For an electromagnetic wave to radiate efficiently into free space without massive dissipation, the transmitting antenna must be roughly one-quarter wavelength long:

Quarter-Wavelength Antenna Law
Lantenna ≈ λ4 = c4 · f
Where c = 3 × 108 m/s is the speed of light in vacuum, and f is signal frequency.
  • Raw Audio (1 kHz): λ = 300 km &implies; L = 75 km tall antenna! Absolutely impossible to build.
  • RF Carrier (100 MHz): λ = 3 m &implies; L = 75 cm practical whip antenna fitted on cars and phones!
📻 Sharing the Medium (Collision Prevention)
  • Baseband Overlap: Every human voice and music track occupies the identical baseband range (20 Hz to 20 kHz).
  • Catastrophic Collision: If multiple stations transmitted raw audio directly, all signals would occupy the exact same spectrum and garble into deafening noise.
  • Carrier Translation: Shifting each station’s audio onto a dedicated high-frequency carrier (fc1, fc2, fc3) allows thousands of broadcasters to share the airwaves cleanly without interference.
  • Band-Pass Physical Media: Coaxial cables, optical fibers, and satellite atmosphere are band-pass channels that heavily attenuate low frequencies near 0 Hz.
Modulation Taxonomy

Three Ways to Carry an Analog Message

Modulating the carrier’s amplitude, frequency, or phase while holding the other two constant
Slide 4 / 23
General Sinusoidal Carrier Wave
c(t) = Ac cos(2πfct + φ)
Three distinct parameters can be varied in proportion to the message m(t): Amplitude (Ac), Frequency (fc), or Phase (φ).
1. Amplitude Modulation (AM)

The carrier’s peak amplitude follows the audio message; its frequency and phase remain strictly constant.

s(t) = Ac [1 + m·m(t)] cos(2πfct)
Used in MW/SW broadcast, aviation radio.
2. Frequency Modulation (FM)

The carrier’s frequency swings with the message; its amplitude and phase remain strictly constant.

f(t) = fc + Δf·m(t)
Used in commercial FM radio, TV sound, two-way radio.
3. Phase Modulation (PM)

The carrier’s phase angle varies with the message; amplitude stays constant. Frequency tracks dm/dt.

s(t) = Ac cos(2πfct + kp·m(t))
Foundational to digital PSK/QAM and satellite telemetry.
Amplitude Modulation

Amplitude Modulation (AM) & The Envelope

Multiplying the carrier by (1 + message) so that the peak outline replicates the sound
Slide 5 / 23
AM Time-Domain Equation
s(t) = Ac [1 + m cos(2πfmt)] · cos(2πfct)
Ac: Unmodulated carrier peak amplitude (Volts)
m: Modulation index (0 ≤ m ≤ 1 for linear modulation)
fm: Modulating message frequency (Hz)
fc: Carrier frequency (Hz), where fc ≫ fm
✨ The Envelope Concept
  • The Envelope: The smooth curve connecting the positive and negative RF peaks.
  • Exact Audio Copy: Because the carrier is multiplied by [1 + m·m(t)], the envelope matches the original message shape.
  • Simplicity: The receiver does not need complex frequency tracking; a simple diode envelope detector extracts the sound.

The Modulation Index m & Overmodulation

Under-modulation ($m < 1$), 100% modulation ($m = 1$), and destructive overmodulation ($m > 1$)
Slide 6 / 23
Modulation Index Formula (Forouzan)
m = Amax − AminAmax + Amin = AmAc
Amax = maximum envelope peak voltage; Amin = minimum envelope trough voltage.
🎛 Adjust Modulation Index
Modulation Index (m) 0.65
✔ Normal AM (m = 0.65): Envelope cleanly mirrors the baseband audio wave.
⚠️ The Three Modulation States
  • m < 1 (Under-modulation): Envelope never touches zero. Safe and clear, but under-utilizes transmitter power.
  • m = 1 (100% Modulation): Envelope touches zero at troughs. Maximum undistorted efficiency.
  • m > 1 (Overmodulation): Envelope crosses zero; carrier inverts phase by 180°. Envelope detector clips the inverted peaks, causing harsh acoustic distortion!

Frequency Domain

AM Spectrum: Carrier & Mirror Sidebands

Frequency translation shifts baseband up to fc, creating Upper and Lower Sidebands
Slide 7 / 23
AM Bandwidth Formula
BAM = (fc + fm) − (fc − fm) = 2fm = 2B
An AM transmission always requires exactly twice the bandwidth of the original baseband audio message.
📊 Spectral Structure of AM
  • Carrier (fc): High-power discrete spike. Carries no audio information on its own.
  • Lower Sideband (LSB): Spans from fc − B up to fc. Contains the inverted audio spectrum.
  • Upper Sideband (USB): Spans from fc up to fc + B. Contains the non-inverted audio spectrum.
  • Redundancy: USB and LSB are identical mirror images. Transmitting both is redundant.
  • Single-Sideband (SSB): Filters out the carrier and one sideband, cutting required bandwidth in half (BSSB = B) and saving massive transmitter power.

Power Distribution

AM Power: Why Broadcast AM Wastes Transmitter Watts

Carrier carries zero information; sidebands hold all intelligence but receive little power
Slide 9 / 23
AM Total Power Formula
Pt = Pc (1 + m22) = Pc + PLSB + PUSB
Pc = Carrier power (unmodulated)
PLSB = PUSB = Pc · m24 (Total sideband power Psb = Pc · m22)
💡 AM Transmitter Power Distribution
  • Carrier Waste: The carrier accounts for at least 66.7% (two-thirds) of total power even at 100% modulation (m = 1).
  • Sideband Starvation: The audio information is stored exclusively in the sidebands, which get only one-third of the power!
  • Why Not Suppress Carrier? Commercial broadcast receivers must remain cheap ($5 pocket radios). A full carrier allows simple diode envelope detection without a local oscillator.
Example 3: Transmitter Power Breakdown Textbook Example
Given:
Carrier power Pc = 100 W, m = 0.8.
Total Power Calculation:
Pt = 100 × (1 + 0.822) = 100 × 1.32 = 132 W
Useful Sideband Power:
Psb = 132 − 100 = 32 W &implies; Efficiency = 32132 ≈ 24.2%
Transmitter Power Efficiency: Only 24.2% of RF energy carries sound!

Frequency Modulation

Frequency Modulation (FM) & The VCO

Carrier frequency swings with the message amplitude while amplitude remains 100% constant
Slide 11 / 23
Instantaneous Frequency Equation
f(t) = fc + Δf · m(t)
fc: Unmodulated center carrier frequency
\Delta f: Peak frequency deviation (maximum swing from fc in Hz)
\beta: FM modulation index: β = Δffm
⚡ The Voltage-Controlled Oscillator (VCO)
  • Hardware Implementation: FM is created using a VCO, where input audio voltage directly alters the capacitance of a varactor diode, tuning the oscillator frequency.
  • Positive Peaks: Carrier cycles compress tightly (frequency increases up to fc + Δf).
  • Negative Troughs: Carrier cycles stretch wide (frequency decreases down to fc − Δf).
  • Constant Amplitude: The peak-to-peak voltage NEVER changes! This is the secret to FM’s immunity to noise.

FM Bandwidth

FM Spectrum & Carson’s Rule

Infinite theoretical Bessel sidebands, but 98% of power is strictly bounded by Carson’s Rule
Slide 12 / 23
Carson’s Bandwidth Rule
BFM ≈ 2(Δf + fm) = 2(1 + β)fm
Contains approximately 98% of total transmitted signal power. Forouzan standard benchmark for broadcast FM uses β = 4.
📐 Bessel Functions Jn(β)
  • Infinite Sidebands: FM creates spectral lines at fc ± fm, fc ± 2fm, fc ± 3fm, …
  • Amplitudes: The height of the n-th sideband is given by |Jn(β)|.
  • Bessel Boundary: Sidebands beyond n = β + 1 drop below 1% of carrier amplitude and can be safely filtered out without causing audible distortion.
  • Trade-Off: Larger β &implies; superior noise immunity, but consumes wider transmission bandwidth.

Noise Immunity

Why FM Sounds Cleaner than AM: Amplitude Limiting

Atmospheric and thermal noise attack amplitude; FM receivers clip noise away with a limiter
Slide 14 / 23
⚡ The Nature of Noise
  • Natural Noise is Additive Amplitude Noise: Lightning strikes, power line hum, ignition sparks, and thermal Johnson noise all corrupt signal amplitude.
  • Why AM Suffers: In AM, the amplitude envelope IS the audio. Any noise spike directly changes the envelope and passes into your loudspeaker as crackling static!
  • Why FM is Immune: In FM, the audio is encoded strictly in the zero-crossing rate (time distance between zero axis crossings). Amplitude variations carry zero information.
  • The Amplitude Limiter: An FM receiver uses a saturating amplifier to hard-clip the incoming signal to a constant flat height, completely stripping away noise spikes before demodulation!

Phase Modulation

Phase Modulation (PM) & The Derivative Rule

Carrier phase tracks message value; frequency shifts in proportion to the message slope
Slide 15 / 23
PM Signal Equation
s(t) = Ac cos(2πfct + kp·m(t))
k_p = phase deviation constant (radians per Volt).
📐 The Derivative Relationship

Because instantaneous frequency is the time derivative of total phase:

fi(t) = 12π dθdt = fc + kp2π · dm(t)dt
  • PM is FM of the Derivative: A PM signal changes frequency where the message changes FASTEST, not where it is largest.
  • At Peaks of Sine Wave: Slope dmdt = 0 &implies; instantaneous frequency equals fc.
  • At Zero-Crossings: Slope dmdt is maximum &implies; frequency swings to its extreme deviation!
  • Bandwidth: Same Carson formula BPM ≈ 2(1 + β)B, but uses smaller \beta (1 to 3).

Bridge to FDM

Many Stations, One Medium: The Bridge to FDM

How modulation enables Frequency-Division Multiplexing (Preview of Lecture 09)
Slide 21 / 23
🌉 Modulation as the Foundation of FDM
  • One Physical Medium: Whether air, coaxial copper cable, or glass fiber, all senders share the same channel.
  • Frequency Separation: Station 1 modulates carrier fc1; Station 2 modulates carrier fc2; Station 3 modulates carrier fc3.
  • Guard Bands: Narrow unused frequency gaps placed between adjacent channels to absorb filter roll-off and prevent cross-talk bleed.
  • Preview of Lecture 09: This process is the exact definition of Frequency-Division Multiplexing (FDM), which we will explore alongside Wavelength-Division Multiplexing (WDM) and Time-Division Multiplexing (TDM) in our next lecture!
